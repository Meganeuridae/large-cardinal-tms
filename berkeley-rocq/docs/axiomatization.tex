{\Large\bfseries ZF plus the existence of a Berkeley cardinal}\\[3pt]
{\small First-order axiomatization for the Berkeley1399 verification package}
\end{center}
\textbf{Language and conventions.}
Classical first-order logic with equality and the sole nonlogical symbol $\in$.
Take universal closures of the displayed axioms. All sets, functions, ordinals,
formula codes, and truth relations below are internal to this set theory.
Neither Choice nor Dependent Choice is added.

\textbf{ZF axioms.} In the two schemas, formulas may have arbitrary finite
tuples of parameters $\bar p$; bound and auxiliary variables are chosen fresh.
Write $x\subseteq a$ for $\forall t(t\in x\to t\in a)$ and $x^+=x\cup\{x\}$.
\begin{align*}
\text{Extensionality:}\quad&[\forall x(x\in a\leftrightarrow x\in b)]\to a=b.\\
\text{Empty set:}\quad&\exists e\,\forall x\ (x\notin e).\\
\text{Pairing:}\quad&\exists c\,\forall x\ [x\in c\leftrightarrow(x=a\lor x=b)].\\
\text{Union:}\quad&\exists b\,\forall x\ [x\in b\leftrightarrow\exists y\in a\ (x\in y)].\\
\text{Power set:}\quad&\exists b\,\forall x\ [x\in b\leftrightarrow x\subseteq a].\\
\text{Infinity:}\quad&\exists a\ [\varnothing\in a\ \land\ \forall x\in a\ (x^+\in a)].\\
\text{Separation:}\quad&\exists b\,\forall x\ [x\in b\leftrightarrow(x\in a\land\varphi(x,\bar p))],
\quad b\notin\mathrm{FV}(\varphi).\\
\text{Replacement:}\quad&[\forall x\in a\ \forall y\,\forall z\
 ((\varphi(x,y,\bar p)\land\varphi(x,z,\bar p))\to y=z)]\\[-2pt]
&\hspace{12mm}\to\exists b\,\forall y\ [y\in b\leftrightarrow\exists x\in a\ \varphi(x,y,\bar p)],
\quad b\notin\mathrm{FV}(\varphi).\\
\text{Foundation:}\quad&a\ne\varnothing\ \to\ \exists x\in a\ \forall y\in x\ (y\notin a).
\end{align*}
Replacement is the bounded-domain, partial-functional form: each input has
\emph{at most} one output. Over the other ZF axioms it is equivalent to the usual
total-functional schema.

\textbf{Set-coded syntax and truth.}
Use Kuratowski pairs $\langle a,b\rangle=\{\{a\},\{a,b\}\}$ and the least inductive
set $\omega$. Let $\FormulaSet$ be the least set closed under the following constructors
($i,k\in\omega$ and $p,q\in\FormulaSet$):
\[
\Eq(i,k)=\langle0,\langle i,k\rangle\rangle,\quad
\Mem(i,k)=\langle1,\langle i,k\rangle\rangle,\quad
\Neg(p)=\langle2,p\rangle,\quad
\Imp(p,q)=\langle3,\langle p,q\rangle\rangle,\quad
\All(i,p)=\langle4,\langle i,p\rangle\rangle.
\]
For a set $M$, let $E_M$ be the set of all function graphs $s:\omega\to M$.
The first-order condition $\Sat_M(S)$ says $S\subseteq\FormulaSet\times E_M$ and the
following five clauses hold for all indicated codes and assignments; abbreviate
$\langle p,s\rangle\in S$ by $S(p,s)$:
\begin{align*}
S(\Eq(i,k),s)&\leftrightarrow s(i)=s(k), &
S(\Mem(i,k),s)&\leftrightarrow s(i)\in s(k),\\
S(\Neg(p),s)&\leftrightarrow\neg S(p,s), &
S(\Imp(p,q),s)&\leftrightarrow(S(p,s)\to S(q,s)),\\
S(\All(i,p),s)&\leftrightarrow\forall x\in M\ S(p,s[i\mapsto x]). &&
\end{align*}
Here $s[i\mapsto x]$ changes just coordinate $i$.
Least closure, function graphs, evaluation, and updates are definable using
$\in$ and $=$; ZF proves the required sets and truth relation exist.

\textbf{The Berkeley axiom $B$.}
Put $\Trans(M)\equiv\forall x\in M\ \forall y\in x\ (y\in M)$.
$\Ord(\delta)$ means $\Trans(\delta)$ and $\in$ is a strict total order on $\delta$
(Foundation supplies well-foundedness).
$\Card(\delta)$ means $\Ord(\delta)$ and there is no bijection from any
$\alpha\in\delta$ onto $\delta$.
The additional axiom is the single sentence
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\exists\delta\ \bigl[\Card(\delta)\land\omega\in\delta\ \land\
\forall M\bigl((\Trans(M)\land\delta\in M)\ \to\ \exists S\,[\Sat_M(S)\ \land\qquad\qquad\\[-2pt]
\forall\eta\in\delta\ \exists j\,\exists\kappa\,[j:M\to M\ \land\
\eta\in\kappa\land\kappa\in\delta\land j(\kappa)\ne\kappa\land
(\forall\alpha\in\kappa\ (j(\alpha)=\alpha))\qquad\\[-2pt]
{}\land\ (\forall p\in\FormulaSet\ \forall s\in E_M\
 (S(p,s)\leftrightarrow S(p,j\circ s)))],]\bigr)\bigr].
\end{aligned}}
\]
Thus $j:(M,\in)\to(M,\in)$ is elementary and $\eta<\operatorname{crit}(j)<\delta$.
One $j$ preserves \emph{all} internally coded formulas. This is a first-order
sentence via the set-coded definitions above, not a separate axiom for each formula.
